Constants and conventions

How each number is calculated
Neutral peptide mass M = Σ(residue masses) + H2O, then −0.98402 Da for a C-terminal amide and +42.01057 Da for an N-terminal acetyl (monoisotopic values shown).
Charge states m/z = (M + Σ carrier masses) / z, where each carrier mass is the neutral atom/molecule mass minus one electron mass (0.000549 Da): H+ 1.007276, Na+ 22.989220, K+ 38.963158, NH4+ 18.033823. Negative mode uses (M − z·1.007276)/z.
Deletions subtract a residue mass (not the free amino acid): residue = amino acid − H2O.
Truncations are recomputed from the remaining sequence, so terminal chemistry is handled explicitly — N-terminal truncations keep the C-terminal group, C-terminal truncations keep the N-terminal group.
Modifications are evaluated from their elemental composition in both monoisotopic and average mode, so switching mass type rescales every row consistently.
Research tool for interpreting crude SPPS LC–MS data. All masses are computed from elemental compositions at run time; verify critical assignments against a standard and against isotope-pattern/charge spacing in the raw data. Works fully offline — save the file locally and open it in any browser.
